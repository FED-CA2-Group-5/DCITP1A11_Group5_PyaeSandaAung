Meal Planning Tips

Four small habits that turn a chaotic week of cooking into one plate at a time, without the last-minute scramble.

0 of 4 tips checked off
01

Plan Your Week First

Pick your meals before the week starts, not the night before. Ten quiet minutes on Sunday saves time, cuts stress, and stops you from ordering takeout out of decision fatigue.

Sun: pasta night · Mon: sheet-pan chicken · Tue: leftovers · Wed: stir-fry · Thu: tacos · Fri: takeout (planned!) · Sat: fridge-clear-out soup
02

Build a Grocery List

Write down every ingredient your planned meals need before you shop. A real list keeps you out of the snack aisle, cuts food waste, and means one trip instead of three.

Group by aisle: Produce (onions, peppers, garlic) · Protein (chicken thighs, eggs) · Pantry (rice, canned tomatoes) · Dairy (cheese, butter)
03

Prep Ahead of Time

Chop vegetables, portion proteins, and mix sauces while you have a free evening. Future you just has to cook, not prep, which makes weeknight dinners twice as fast.

Sunday, 30 minutes: dice onions + peppers into one container, portion chicken into meal-size bags, whisk up one batch of marinade.
04

Store Meals the Right Way

Cool cooked food before sealing it, use airtight containers, and label what goes in the freezer. Proper storage keeps leftovers safe and tasting fresh for days, not just hours.

Fridge: 3–4 days, labeled with the date. Freezer: up to 3 months in a flat, sealed bag so it thaws fast.
